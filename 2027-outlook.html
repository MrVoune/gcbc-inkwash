GCBC 2027

Bridging Two Worlds

Business Decisions in an Evolving U.S.-China Relationship

万里通衢，顺势谋新：中美关系变动下的商业机遇与选择

The Greater China Business Conference (GCBC) 2027 will examine how businesses make decisions as the U.S.-China relationship reshapes the global landscape. Competition, interdependence, and uncertainty coexist. The central question is practical: how can companies draw on the distinct strengths of both markets, navigate the limits of cross-border activity, and make sound choices when commercial opportunity and geopolitical pressure meet? The focus is business decisions, not political debate or purely historical inquiry.

Why This Theme

GCBC has long brought together a wide range of voices connected to China. This year, we want a clearer thread across those conversations. Focusing on the evolving U.S.-China relationship gives speakers from different fields a shared question to address and helps attendees see how its effects vary across industries. As a leading global business school, Kellogg provides a platform for people with different backgrounds and perspectives to exchange ideas.

Audience & Takeaways

GCBC 2027 is designed for business professionals, entrepreneurs, investors, and students who want to understand how the U.S.-China relationship shapes business opportunities and decisions. By connecting historical and macroeconomic context with perspectives from different industries, attendees will leave with a clearer sense of which cross-border opportunities to pursue, what risks to weigh, and how to draw on the strengths of both markets.

How the Conversation Will Unfold

The conference will feature four panels. History and Macroeconomics will establish the broader context, while three industry panels will examine how the U.S.-China relationship shapes business decisions in their respective sectors.

01 · Context

History & Macroeconomics

How historical, geopolitical, and economic forces have shaped U.S.-China relations, what defines the relationship today, and which future paths businesses should consider.

02 · Industry

Technology

How technology companies, particularly those working in AI, draw on capabilities across the U.S. and China while navigating questions of market access, competition, and collaboration.

03 · Industry

Healthcare

How healthcare companies translate innovation into products and services across the U.S. and China, and how regulatory and market differences shape commercialization and collaboration.

04 · Industry

Retail & Culture

How consumer brands, entertainment, and cultural products reach audiences across markets, and how companies respond to local preferences and perceptions within the U.S.-China context.
